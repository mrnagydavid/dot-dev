Look up words, learn languages.

A dictionary for language learners that fits on one phone screen. Type a word, pick a language, and read its Wiktionary entry. When Wiktionary is not enough, bab.la is one tap away.

Open Lemma (opens in a new tab) Free · No account · English, Swedish, Spanish, French

Why it exists

Looking up word after word on the Wiktionary website from a phone was hard work. Maybe it was just me, but I kept editing the URL by hand, because that felt faster than the search.

When Wiktionary was not enough, I went to bab.la for example sentences, and to hear the word read out loud. And Wiktionary does not always show the pronunciation in IPA.

Lemma puts all of that on one screen: a search bar under your thumb, Wiktionary and bab.la side by side, and the IPA for the word.

What it does

Search where your thumb is

The search bar sits at the bottom of the screen. The keyboard closes after each lookup, so the next word is one tap away.

Jump to your language

Pick English, Swedish, Spanish or French. Lemma opens that language's part of the entry.

bab.la, one tap away

The second tab opens the same word on bab.la, with example sentences and audio.

Pronunciation

The IPA shows above the entry, also when Wiktionary has none. It comes from ipa-dict.

A link for every lookup

Bookmark a lookup or send it to someone: lemma.mrnagydavid.dev/?word=perro&lang=es

Remembers and installs

Lemma remembers your language. Add it to your home screen and it opens like an app.

Privacy policy, in full

Known limits

  • The entries need a connection. Only the app itself works offline.
  • Four languages for now.
  • The entries belong to Wiktionary and bab.la. When they change their pages, what you see in Lemma can change too.
  • On Firefox for Android, the toolbar moves to the top of the screen.
